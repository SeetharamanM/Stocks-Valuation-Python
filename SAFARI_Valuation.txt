Objective

Conduct a focused valuation of the stock and arrive at a defensible fair value estimate,
target price and investment recommendation. This is NOT a full company analysis — it is a
deep-dive on valuation alone. Assume the reader already knows the business; spend the
entire report on what the company is worth, what the market is currently pricing in, and
whether the gap between the two is actionable.

Source & Outline of Analysis

Take financial statements and data points from Screener.in. Cross-reference Screener.in,
Yahoo Finance and TradingView for the most recent price close, market capitalisation and
peer multiples. Adhere STRICTLY to the sample output outline below. Charts and
visualisations are encouraged, particularly the valuation band chart and the sensitivity
grid.

Instructions

Take input from User — Valuation & Recommendation Report

By SEETHARAMAN

1. Valuation Snapshot

-   Company name, sector, current market price, market capitalisation, 52-week range.

-   Recommendation box (quick reference, derived from Section 9 below):

    -   Recommendation: STRONG BUY / BUY / HOLD / SELL / STRONG SELL

    -   Fair Value: Rs x

    -   Target Price: Rs x (y% upside/downside)

    -   Accumulation Zone: Rs a - Rs b

    -   Investment Horizon: [n] months

    -   Conviction: High / Medium / Low, with a one-line reason

-   A single-paragraph verdict stating the core valuation argument in plain language.

2. Valuation Base — the inputs everything else rests on

Before any multiple or model, establish and disclose the base figures. State clearly
whether each is standalone or consolidated, and which fiscal period it is drawn from.

-   Earnings base: reported PAT and EPS (latest FY and TTM)

-   Normalised earnings: strip out exceptional items, one-off other income, and any
    non-recurring tax benefit. Show the adjustment as a line-item bridge from reported
    to normalised EPS. If exceptional items have appeared in four of the last five years,
    treat them as recurring and say so.

-   Book value per share, and tangible book value per share

-   Net debt or net cash per share (Total debt - Cash & equivalents & investments)

-   Diluted share count, including outstanding ESOPs; note any buyback in progress

-   Effective tax rate versus statutory (~25.17%), and whether any SEZ or concessional
    benefit is scheduled to expire

-   Free cash flow: CFO - Capex, for each of the last five years, and FCF/PAT conversion

3. Relative Valuation — where the stock trades today

Present a table of current multiples alongside the company's own 5-year and 10-year
median, and alongside the peer set:

-   P/E (trailing and forward where consensus is available)

-   P/B, and P/Tangible Book

-   EV/EBITDA and EV/EBIT (report both — EBITDA alone hides capital intensity)

-   EV/Sales and P/Sales

-   PEG (P/E divided by expected earnings growth)

-   Dividend yield, and total shareholder yield including buybacks

-   Free cash flow yield (FCF / Market cap)

For the peer comparison, name three to five genuine comparables from the same sector and
of broadly comparable size. Do not compare across business models. For each peer show at
minimum P/E, ROCE (or ROE for financials) and Debt/Equity, plus the peer median.

Then answer explicitly: is the current premium or discount to peers and to own history
justified by a difference in growth, returns on capital, or risk? Quantify where possible.

4. Historical Valuation Bands

-   Compute the 5-year and 10-year median P/E (and the sector-appropriate primary multiple,
    e.g. P/B for banks, EV/EBITDA for capital-intensive businesses).

-   Show the +1 and -1 standard deviation bands around the median.

-   State where the current multiple sits within that range, in standard deviations.

-   Flag whether the historical band is still a valid anchor — a structural change in
    growth rate, margin profile, regulation or capital structure invalidates the mean-
    reversion assumption. Say so plainly if the past band should be discarded.

-   Where a re-rating or de-rating has occurred, identify the year it happened and the
    cause.

5. Intrinsic Valuation — the primary model

Select the model that fits the business and justify the choice in one sentence.

a) Discounted Cash Flow (default for non-financials)

-   Explicit forecast period: 5 years, with revenue growth, EBIT margin, tax rate,
    capex and working-capital change stated for each year

-   Cross-check the growth assumption against the arithmetic ceiling:
    Sustainable Growth Rate = ROE x (1 - Payout Ratio), and
    Expected g = Reinvestment Rate x ROIC.
    If the model's assumed growth exceeds both, the model is incoherent — reconcile it or
    lower the assumption.

-   Cost of equity via CAPM: risk-free rate (10-yr G-Sec), equity risk premium, and beta,
    with each number sourced and stated

-   WACC, with the debt/equity weights and post-tax cost of debt shown

-   Terminal value: state the method (perpetuity growth or exit multiple), the terminal
    growth rate (should not exceed long-run nominal GDP, ~5-6% for India), and what
    percentage of total value sits in the terminal value. If terminal value exceeds 70%
    of enterprise value, flag it — the valuation is an assumption about the far future,
    not an analysis of the near one.

-   Bridge from enterprise value to equity value to per-share fair value

-   A 5x5 sensitivity grid: WACC on one axis, terminal growth on the other

b) Excess Return / Justified Multiple (for banks and NBFCs)

For a bank, discard DCF, ROCE, EV/EBITDA and capex-based measures entirely. Use:

    Justified P/B = (ROE - g) / (COE - g)

Supported by: sustainable ROE, ROA, NIM, CASA ratio, credit cost, slippage ratio, PCR,
CRAR, cost-to-income. Also compute the Internal Capital Generation Rate = ROE x (1 - payout);
if advances are growing faster than this, equity dilution is coming and must be priced in.

c) Sum-of-the-Parts (for conglomerates and multi-segment businesses)

Value each segment on its own appropriate multiple, state the multiple and the basis,
apply a holding-company discount where relevant, and disclose it.

d) Dividend Discount Model (for mature, high-payout, low-growth businesses)

Use where payout exceeds 60% and growth is stable.

Note for asset managers: value on P/E and P/AUM, and decompose revenue growth into
AUM growth plus yield change — AUM can compound while revenue does not.

6. Reverse DCF — what the market is already paying for

Take the current market price as given and solve for the growth rate the market is
implicitly assuming, holding margins, WACC and terminal growth at reasonable levels.

Report a single number: the implied growth rate priced in.

Then compare it against your own estimate from Section 5, and against the arithmetic
ceiling from SGR and Reinvestment x ROIC.

This is the most important paragraph in the report. State whether the market's implied
expectation is achievable, conservative or heroic, and why.

7. Fair Value Triangulation

Blend the methods into a single fair value estimate in a table:

    Method | Fair value per share | Weight | Weighted contribution | Rationale for weight

Weight according to how well each method suits the business. Justify each weight in one
line. Show the resulting blended fair value, and the range (lowest to highest method
output) as the honest uncertainty band.

8. Scenario Analysis

Three scenarios, each with stated assumptions and an assigned probability:

    Scenario | Key assumptions | Fair value | Upside/Downside | Probability

-   Bear: what has to go wrong, and what the stock is worth if it does

-   Base: the central case

-   Bull: what has to go right

Compute the probability-weighted expected value and compare it to the current price.
An asymmetric payoff (limited downside, meaningful upside) matters more than a high base-
case target.

9. Recommendation

-   Rating: STRONG BUY / BUY / HOLD / SELL / STRONG SELL

-   Target price, and the multiple or method it is derived from

-   Time horizon to target, with reasoning for that horizon

-   Accumulation zone: the price range at which the risk-reward is attractive

-   Review trigger: the specific price or event at which the thesis should be re-examined

-   Position-sizing note: whether this warrants a full, half or starter position given the
    conviction level and the valuation uncertainty band

Rationale: five to seven numbered points, each anchored to a figure computed earlier in
this report. No qualitative assertion without a number behind it.

10. Risks to the Valuation

-   The three assumptions the fair value is most sensitive to, ranked, with the fair value
    impact of each being wrong by a stated margin

-   Sector-specific and regulatory risks that would compress the multiple

-   What specifically would invalidate the thesis — name the observable event, not a vague
    concern

11. Conclusion

Restate the valuation argument, the fair value, the recommendation and the horizon in
under 200 words. No new information.

Output Type

Produce a single standalone interactive HTML file that can be opened directly in any
browser without a build step. Do not produce a markdown report. Do not produce a React JSX
file. The final deliverable must be a .html file saved to outputs. Design as a professional
interactive report with the following features:

Professional Design Elements:

-   Clean, corporate colour scheme with professional typography

-   Branded header with company name, current price and report title

-   Prominent recommendation box at the top carrying the rating, fair value, target price
    and horizon

-   Colour-coded valuation signals: undervalued in green, fairly valued in orange,
    overvalued in red

-   Proper spacing and margins for readability

-   Each of the eleven sections should be a separate tab in the UI, not one long scroll

Required Visualisations:

-   Valuation band chart: multiple over the last 5-10 years with median and +/-1 SD lines,
    and the current level marked

-   Peer comparison bar chart on the primary multiple

-   DCF sensitivity heat map (WACC vs terminal growth), colour-scaled

-   Scenario chart showing bear/base/bull fair values against the current price

-   Fair value triangulation chart showing each method's output as a range, with the
    blended estimate marked

Well-Structured Content:

-   All eleven sections maintained

-   Data presented in neatly formatted tables

-   Clear hierarchical headings and subheadings

-   Every computed figure shown with its formula available on hover or in a footnote

-   Page breaks at logical points for PDF printing

-   Footer with disclaimer and data-source attribution

Important Notes

-   For Indian markets the financial year runs 1 April to 31 March. FY26 is
    1 April 2025 to 31 March 2026.

-   Keep the lookback period at the last 5 fiscal years for financial data, and 10 years
    for valuation bands where available.

-   For all Balance Sheet, Income Statement and Cash Flow data, use the latest available
    annual period. For price and market metrics, use the latest available close, and state
    the date of that close.

-   State every assumption explicitly. A valuation whose assumptions are hidden cannot be
    checked or updated.

-   Where a data point is unavailable, say so and provide a reasoned qualitative
    assessment — do not silently omit it and do not fabricate a figure.

-   Distinguish clearly between figures taken from source and figures computed by you.

-   Use average shareholders' equity for ROE, not closing equity.

-   Include buybacks in the payout ratio, not dividends alone — this materially affects
    SGR for Indian IT companies.

-   Do not apply ROCE, EV/EBITDA, capex intensity or cash-conversion-cycle metrics to banks,
    NBFCs or asset managers. Use the sector-specific set in Section 5(b).

-   Maintain an objective tone. Present the case against the recommendation as well as for it.

-   Use bold for headings and subheadings. Highlight or underline the fair value, target
    price and final recommendation.

-   No need to mention the report generation date.

-   Close with the standard disclaimer: analytical method only, not investment advice.

Reference Output

Reliance Industries Ltd — Valuation & Recommendation Report

By Rahul Chandra

1. Valuation Snapshot

Current Price: Rs 1,248 | Market Cap: Rs 16,88,704 Cr | 52-week range: Rs 1,115 - Rs 1,609

Recommendation: BUY
Fair Value: Rs 1,850
Target Price: Rs 1,850 (48% upside)
Accumulation Zone: Rs 1,180 - Rs 1,290
Investment Horizon: 18-24 months
Conviction: Medium — the SOTP gap is wide, but three of the four segments are valued on
peer multiples rather than cash flows.

Verdict: The market is pricing RIL close to a standalone O2C business with a modest
premium, and is not paying for the digital and retail segments at anything near the
multiples comparable listed peers command. The gap is a segment-valuation gap, not an
earnings-growth gap.

2. Valuation Base

  Item                          Value
  ----------------------------- ---------------------
  Reported EPS (FY24)           Rs 51.1
  Less: exceptional items       Rs (1.8)
  Normalised EPS                Rs 49.3
  Book value per share          Rs 590
  Net debt per share            Rs 176
  Diluted share count           676.6 Cr
  Effective tax rate            24.1% (vs 25.17%)
  FCF/PAT (3-yr avg)            41% — below the 70% healthy threshold; capex-driven

Commentary: The low FCF conversion is the single most important input. It is explained by
the new-energy and retail capex cycle rather than by working capital deterioration, which
is a materially different situation, but it does mean a DCF is highly sensitive to when
that capex normalises.

3. Relative Valuation

  Multiple        Current   5-yr median   Peer median   Read
  --------------- --------- ------------- ------------- ------------------
  P/E             24.4      22.1          20.6          Premium
  P/B             2.12      1.94          1.85          Modest premium
  EV/EBITDA       11.8      10.9          7.4           Significant premium
  EV/EBIT         18.2      16.4          10.1          Significant premium
  FCF yield       1.9%      2.4%          5.1%          Poor

  Peer            P/E     ROCE      Debt/Equity
  --------------- ------- --------- -------------
  RIL             24.41   9.61%     0.44
  IOCL            18.28   21.14%    0.90
  BPCL            8.19    32.09%    0.76
  HPCL            11.42   21.26%    1.58
  Peer median     20.55   21.45%    0.78

Is the premium justified? Partly. RIL earns roughly half the ROCE of its refining peers,
which on its own argues for a discount, not a premium. The premium is justified only if the
consumer businesses are worth materially more than the consolidated numbers suggest — which
is exactly the proposition the SOTP tests.

4. Historical Valuation Bands

10-year median P/E of 22.1, with +1 SD at 28.4 and -1 SD at 15.8. The current 24.4 sits
0.4 SD above median — mid-band, neither cheap nor stretched on its own history.

The band remains a valid anchor, but with a caveat: the business mix in FY15 was ~85% O2C
and is now ~57%, so the older half of the series describes a different company. The 5-year
band is the more reliable reference.

5. Intrinsic Valuation — Sum-of-the-Parts

  Segment           Basis            Multiple   Value/share
  ----------------- ---------------- ---------- ------------
  O2C               FY24 EBITDA      9x         Rs 820
  Jio Platforms     FY24 EBITDA      20x        Rs 540
  Reliance Retail   FY24 EBITDA      25x        Rs 420
  New Energy etc.   Invested capital 1x         Rs 70
  ----------------- ---------------- ---------- ------------
  Gross SOTP value                              Rs 1,850

SOTP chosen over DCF because the four segments have materially different growth, margin and
capital-intensity profiles, and a single blended WACC would misprice all four. The weakness
of the method is that it inherits whatever error sits in the peer multiples used.

6. Reverse DCF

At Rs 1,248, holding WACC at 11.2% and terminal growth at 5%, the market is implying
consolidated EBIT growth of approximately 8% per annum over the next decade.

Against the arithmetic ceiling: ROE of 9.25% with a payout near 9% gives an SGR of roughly
8.4%. Reinvestment rate x ROIC gives a similar figure. The market's implied 8% is therefore
achievable without external funding and is not a heroic assumption — it is close to the
company's own self-funding ceiling.

This is the crux: the market is not over-paying for growth. The disagreement is about
segment mix, not growth rate.

7. Fair Value Triangulation

  Method                  Fair value   Weight   Contribution   Rationale
  ----------------------- ------------ -------- -------------- --------------------------
  SOTP                    Rs 1,850     50%      Rs 925         Best fit for segment mix
  Relative (peer P/E)     Rs 1,050     20%      Rs 210         Penalises low ROCE
  DCF (consolidated)      Rs 1,620     20%      Rs 324         Blended WACC is a weakness
  P/B x justified ROE     Rs 1,180     10%      Rs 118         Weak fit, non-financial
  ----------------------- ------------ -------- -------------- --------------------------
  Blended fair value                            Rs 1,577

Range across methods: Rs 1,050 to Rs 1,850 — a wide band, and honest disclosure of it
matters more than the point estimate.

8. Scenario Analysis

  Scenario   Key assumptions                              Fair value   Move    Prob.
  ---------- -------------------------------------------- ------------ ------- -------
  Bear       Retail multiple compresses to 15x, new
             energy capex written down                    Rs 1,020     -18%    25%
  Base       Segments hold current multiples, capex
             cycle peaks FY26                             Rs 1,577     +26%    55%
  Bull       Jio or Retail IPO crystallises value at
             or above assumed multiples                   Rs 1,980     +59%    20%

Probability-weighted expected value: Rs 1,514, or +21% against the current price. The
payoff is favourably asymmetric.

9. Recommendation

Rating: BUY
Target Price: Rs 1,850 (SOTP-derived)
Horizon: 18-24 months — the timeframe over which a value-unlocking event or the capex
peak would become visible
Accumulation Zone: Rs 1,180 - Rs 1,290
Review Trigger: a close above Rs 1,700, or any quarter in which retail EBITDA margin
contracts sequentially for a second consecutive period
Position Sizing: half position, added to on confirmation — the Rs 1,050 to Rs 1,850
valuation band is too wide to justify full sizing on the point estimate

Rationale:

1.  Probability-weighted expected value of Rs 1,514 versus a price of Rs 1,248 — a 21%
    expected return with a 25%-probability downside of only 18%.

2.  Reverse DCF implies 8% growth, which is within the company's own SGR of 8.4% — the
    market is not paying up for growth that has to be delivered.

3.  The SOTP gap of 48% is concentrated in two segments with listed comparables, making
    the assumption testable rather than speculative.

4.  Net debt per share of Rs 176 against Rs 590 of book value leaves balance-sheet capacity
    intact through the capex cycle.

5.  Against all of this: FCF/PAT of 41% is genuinely poor, ROCE of 9.61% is less than half
    the peer median, and both must improve for the thesis to work. This is the strongest
    argument for a HOLD rather than a BUY.

10. Risks to the Valuation

-   Retail EBITDA multiple: each 5x of compression removes roughly Rs 84 per share. This is
    the single largest sensitivity and rests entirely on peer multiples holding.

-   Capex normalisation timing: a two-year delay in the capex peak cuts the DCF-derived
    value by approximately 12%.

-   O2C margin cycle: a return to trough refining spreads would remove around Rs 180 per
    share from the O2C leg.

-   Thesis invalidation: an explicit deferral of the Jio or Retail listing beyond FY28, or
    two consecutive quarters of retail margin contraction.

11. Conclusion

RIL trades at Rs 1,248 against a blended fair value of Rs 1,577 and an SOTP value of
Rs 1,850. The reverse DCF shows the market implying 8% growth — within the company's
self-funding capacity, and therefore not a demanding assumption. The disagreement between
price and value is about how the consumer segments are capitalised, not about earnings
delivery. The counter-case is real: returns on capital are half those of refining peers
and cash conversion is weak, and both need to improve. On a probability-weighted expected
value of Rs 1,514 and an asymmetric payoff, a BUY rating with a Rs 1,850 target over
18-24 months is warranted, taken as a half position with additions on confirmation.
